About your teacher

Hi, I’m Lena — your personal English teacher

I qualified as a teacher of English in 2007 and have been teaching ever since. My master’s degree is in language teaching — English as my main subject, German as my second — and in 2016 my teaching qualification was recognised here in the Netherlands, where I have taught Dutch alongside English ever since.

I work the way I wish someone had taught me: you talk, I guide. Lessons run in the language you are learning, from the first minute — no translating back and forth, no school desks, no fear of mistakes. A conversation that happens to be a lesson.

Besides lessons, I work with business correspondence — e-mails, offers, official letters — and I translate, including for the municipality. That’s my specialty: my students don’t just speak better English, they write messages that get answers.

Today I teach English to international students, run the Dutch civic-integration course (KNM), and build language courses for companies — factories, logistics, healthcare and IT — as well as writing reference letters and preparing people for job interviews.

Good to know

Frequently asked questions

How much do business English lessons cost?

An individual lesson runs 70 minutes, a group lesson 75 — and 90 from six participants, so everyone gets checked properly. A single lesson is €45. Translation and support with business correspondence are €55 per hour. Group and in-company prices depend on the size of the group and how demanding the subject is; you get the figure at the intake call.

Do you teach groups at companies?

Yes — at your workplace in Drenthe and Groningen. Practical sessions built around your team’s real work: meetings, customer contact, e-mails. Send a short message about your team and I’ll reply with a proposal within 24 hours.

Can I learn business English online effectively?

Yes — for working adults it often beats a classroom. You speak for most of the lesson instead of waiting for your turn, we rehearse your real meetings and rewrite your real e-mails, and lessons fit around your work day.

Is there a trial lesson?

No — I don’t give trial lessons. What I do give is a free 15-minute intake call: not a lesson, but a conversation in which we work out your goal together, I hear where your English is now, and you hear the rate, the length of the course and how often we would meet. Nothing is committed until you say yes.

Which language do we speak during the lesson?

The one you are learning — from the first minute. I work with a communicative method: we don’t fall back on your own language in the lesson, and the vocabulary of your field is built into the topic we are working on. Outside the lesson I speak Dutch, Russian and Ukrainian too, so the intake call, your questions and any translation work can happen in whichever language suits you.
